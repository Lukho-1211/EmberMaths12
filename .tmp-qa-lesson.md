# Quadratic Sequences

A quadratic sequence is a number pattern where the second difference between consecutive terms is constant.

The general term of a quadratic sequence can be written as Tn = an^2 + bn + c.

To find the first differences, subtract consecutive terms from each other carefully.

When the second differences are constant and equal to 2a, you can solve for a using that fact.

Example: for the sequence 3, 6, 11, 18, 27 the second differences are constant and equal to 2.

Always check your formula by substituting n equals 1 to recover the first term of the sequence.

Revision tip: practise finding a, b and c from three given terms before attempting exam questions.
